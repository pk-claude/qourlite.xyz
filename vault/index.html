The ranking axis is agreement, not quality

Number one is a film almost nobody argues about. Number one hundred is a film almost everybody argued about, and is here anyway because it did one thing better than anyone had. The further down you read, the more you would have to defend out loud.

One claim each

Every film carries a single claim -- the one thing it is on this list for. Screenplay. Practical effects. Choreography. Sound. Ideas. A film with no claim does not make the list no matter how much fun it is.

And one case against

Every entry also carries the strongest argument that it does not belong. Not false modesty -- the actual objection, stated as well as its critics state it. If you cannot answer it, you do not really hold the opinion.

The artifacts

Each film's image is an original 3D render, built and lit from scratch for this page. The object is that film's claim made physical -- a machined instrument for screenplay, a lens for cinematography, a failing truss for stunts -- seeded from the film itself so no two are alike. No posters, no stills, nothing borrowed.

Three watch states

Seen means you watched it and you remember it. Hazy means you know you watched it, probably twenty years ago, and you could not argue about it today -- a rewatch candidate. Unseen is unseen. Tap a button to set it; tap the same one again to clear it.

How your list follows you

Type your first name at the top. States save instantly on the device you are on, then sync so the same name on your phone shows the same list. Different name, different list. There is no password -- anyone who types your first name here sees your list. Treat it like the floor leaderboard, not a diary.
